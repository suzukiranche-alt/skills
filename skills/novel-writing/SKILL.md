# Novel Writing Workflow

This skill provides a structured workflow for guiding users through collaborative novel creation. Act as an active creative partner, walking users through six stages: Concept Development, Character Creation, World-Building, Plot Structure, Drafting, and Revision.

## When to Offer This Workflow

**Trigger conditions:**
- User mentions writing fiction: "write a novel", "create a story", "write fiction"
- User mentions story elements: "develop characters", "build a world", "plot my story"
- User seems to be starting a substantial creative writing project

**Initial offer:**
Offer the user a structured workflow for co-writing the novel. Explain the six stages:

1. **Concept Development**: Define the core idea, genre, themes, and tone
2. **Character Creation**: Develop protagonist, antagonist, and supporting cast
3. **World-Building**: Establish setting, rules, and atmosphere
4. **Plot Structure**: Design the story arc and major plot points
5. **Drafting**: Write scenes and chapters collaboratively
6. **Revision**: Refine prose, pacing, and emotional impact

Ask if they want to follow this workflow or prefer to work freeform. If user declines, work freeform. If user accepts, proceed to Stage 1.

## Stage 1: Concept Development

**Goal:** Establish the foundation of the story - what it's about and why it matters.

### Initial Questions

Start by asking the user for the story's foundation:

1. What's the core idea or premise? (one sentence summary)
2. What genre? (fantasy, sci-fi, romance, thriller, literary fiction, etc.)
3. What themes do you want to explore? (love, redemption, power, identity, etc.)
4. What's the intended tone? (dark, humorous, hopeful, melancholic, etc.)
5. What's the target length? (short story, novella, full novel)
6. Who's the intended audience? (young adult, adult, literary readers, etc.)
7. Any inspirations or comparable works?

Inform them they can answer in shorthand or dump ideas however works best.

### Idea Exploration

If the user doesn't have a clear concept:
- Brainstorm 5-10 premises based on their interests
- Ask what elements excite them most
- Help refine vague ideas into concrete premises

### Concept Document

Once the concept is clear, create a concept document:

```markdown
# [Working Title]

## Premise
[One-paragraph summary]

## Genre & Tone
- Genre: [Genre]
- Tone: [Tone descriptors]
- Target length: [Word count / chapter count]

## Core Themes
- [Theme 1]: [How it manifests]
- [Theme 2]: [How it manifests]

## Unique Hook
[What makes this story different]
```

**Transition:**
Ask if they're happy with the concept or want to refine it further. When ready, proceed to Stage 2.

## Stage 2: Character Creation

**Goal:** Develop compelling, three-dimensional characters that drive the story.

### Protagonist Development

Start with the main character:

1. What's their name and basic demographics?
2. What do they want most? (external goal)
3. What do they need but don't realize? (internal arc)
4. What's their greatest fear?
5. What's their fatal flaw?
6. What's their greatest strength?
7. What's their background/backstory?
8. What makes readers care about them?

### Antagonist Development

Develop the opposing force:

1. Who or what opposes the protagonist?
2. What are their motivations? (make them understandable)
3. How do they challenge the protagonist's flaw?
4. What makes them formidable?
5. Do they have sympathetic qualities?

### Supporting Cast

For each major supporting character:

1. What role do they play? (mentor, ally, love interest, etc.)
2. How do they challenge or support the protagonist?
3. What's their own desire and arc?
4. What makes them distinct?

### Character Relationship Map

Create a visual map of character relationships:
- Who knows whom?
- What are the tensions?
- How do relationships evolve?

### Character Document

Compile character profiles in a structured document.

**Transition:**
Ask if the cast feels complete. When ready, proceed to Stage 3.

## Stage 3: World-Building

**Goal:** Create an immersive setting that enhances the story.

### Setting Foundation

Ask about the world:

1. Where and when is the story set?
2. What's the scope? (single location, city, country, universe)
3. What makes this setting unique?
4. What are the rules? (magic systems, technology, social structures)
5. What's the atmosphere/mood of the world?

### World Details

Explore relevant details based on genre:

**For Fantasy/Sci-Fi:**
- Magic/technology systems and limitations
- Political structures and power dynamics
- History and mythology
- Different cultures or species

**For Contemporary/Literary:**
- Specific locations and their significance
- Social dynamics and class structures
- Cultural context and time period
- Symbolic spaces

**For Historical:**
- Historical accuracy requirements
- Period-specific details
- What liberties to take

### Sensory Details

For each major location:
- What do characters see?
- What do they hear?
- What do they smell?
- What's the emotional feel?

### World Document

Create a world-building reference document organized by relevance to the story.

**Transition:**
Ask if the world feels realized enough. When ready, proceed to Stage 4.

## Stage 4: Plot Structure

**Goal:** Design a compelling narrative arc with satisfying structure.

### Story Structure Selection

Discuss structure options:

1. **Three-Act Structure**: Setup, Confrontation, Resolution
2. **Hero's Journey**: 12-stage mythic structure
3. **Seven-Point Structure**: Hook, Plot Turn 1, Pinch 1, Midpoint, Pinch 2, Plot Turn 2, Resolution
4. **Kishotenketsu**: Introduction, Development, Twist, Conclusion (no conflict-based)
5. **Custom**: Organic structure based on the story's needs

Ask which approach resonates, or suggest one based on the genre.

### Major Plot Points

Define key moments:

1. **Opening Image**: Where does the story begin?
2. **Inciting Incident**: What disrupts the protagonist's world?
3. **First Act Turn**: What commits them to the journey?
4. **Midpoint**: What revelation changes everything?
5. **Dark Night**: What's the lowest point?
6. **Climax**: How does the protagonist face their greatest challenge?
7. **Resolution**: How does the world look after?

### Subplot Integration

For each subplot:
- How does it connect to the main plot?
- How does it enhance themes?
- When does it resolve relative to the main plot?

### Chapter Outline

Create a chapter-by-chapter outline:

```markdown
## Chapter [N]: [Title]
**POV**: [Character]
**Location**: [Setting]
**Timeline**: [When]

### Summary
[What happens in this chapter]

### Purpose
- Plot: [Advances plot by...]
- Character: [Develops character by...]
- Theme: [Explores theme of...]

### Key Scenes
1. [Scene 1 summary]
2. [Scene 2 summary]

### Chapter End Hook
[What makes readers turn the page]
```

**Transition:**
Review the full outline. Ask if the structure feels solid. When ready, proceed to Stage 5.

## Stage 5: Drafting

**Goal:** Write the actual prose, scene by scene.

### Drafting Approach

Ask about their preferred drafting style:

1. **Sequential**: Write from beginning to end
2. **Key Scenes First**: Write pivotal moments, then connect
3. **Character Arcs**: Follow each character's journey
4. **Discovery**: Let scenes emerge organically within structure

### Scene Writing

For each scene:

1. Clarify the scene's purpose and beats
2. Ask about POV and tense
3. Brainstorm opening lines (5-10 options)
4. Draft the scene collaboratively
5. Refine based on feedback

### Writing Collaboration Modes

Offer different collaboration styles:

**Full Draft Mode:**
- Write complete scenes based on the outline
- User provides feedback for revision
- Iterate until satisfied

**Collaborative Mode:**
- Write in alternating sections
- User provides direction, Claude executes
- Real-time feedback loop

**Consultation Mode:**
- User writes, Claude provides feedback
- Suggestions for improvement
- Help with specific challenges (dialogue, description, etc.)

### Scene Elements Checklist

For each scene, ensure:
- [ ] Clear goal for POV character
- [ ] Conflict or tension present
- [ ] Sensory details grounding the scene
- [ ] Character voice consistent
- [ ] Scene advances plot or character
- [ ] Strong ending that propels forward

### Handling Writer's Block

If user gets stuck:
- Brainstorm 10 possible directions
- Skip ahead to a different scene
- Explore character motivation deeper
- Try a different POV or tense
- Free-write without judgment

### Progress Tracking

Maintain a progress document:
- Chapters completed
- Current word count
- Scenes to revisit
- Notes for revision

**Transition:**
When draft is complete (or substantial portion), ask about revision priorities.

## Stage 6: Revision

**Goal:** Transform the draft into polished prose.

### Revision Layers

Work through revision in layers:

**Layer 1: Structure**
- Does each chapter serve its purpose?
- Is the pacing effective?
- Are there gaps or redundancies?
- Does the ending satisfy?

**Layer 2: Character**
- Are voices distinct and consistent?
- Do arcs complete satisfyingly?
- Are motivations clear?
- Do relationships feel authentic?

**Layer 3: Prose**
- Is the writing clear and engaging?
- Are there crutch words or phrases?
- Does dialogue sound natural?
- Are descriptions vivid but not overwrought?

**Layer 4: Polish**
- Grammar and punctuation
- Consistency (names, timeline, details)
- Word choice refinement
- Rhythm and flow

### Revision Techniques

Offer specific revision tools:

**Read-Through Analysis:**
Identify weak sections, plot holes, pacing issues

**Line-by-Line Editing:**
Tighten prose, vary sentence structure, strengthen verbs

**Dialogue Polish:**
Make each character's voice distinct, remove on-the-nose dialogue

**Opening/Closing Review:**
Strengthen chapter openings and endings for maximum impact

### Beta Reader Simulation

Offer to read the manuscript as a fresh reader:
- Flag confusion points
- Note emotional responses
- Identify pacing issues
- Highlight strengths

**Completion:**
When revision is complete, celebrate the achievement. Discuss next steps:
- Additional beta readers
- Professional editing
- Submission/publication options
- Starting the next project

## Tips for Effective Novel Collaboration

**Tone:**
- Be encouraging but honest
- Respect the user's creative vision
- Offer options rather than directives
- Celebrate progress

**Creative Support:**
- Generate options when user is stuck
- Ask "what if" questions to spark ideas
- Connect story elements to themes
- Point out strengths as well as areas for improvement

**Pacing:**
- Let user control the speed
- Don't rush through stages
- Allow time for ideas to develop
- Take breaks when needed

**Manuscript Management:**
- Save work frequently
- Maintain organized files
- Track versions
- Keep notes on decisions made

**Quality Focus:**
- Every scene should earn its place
- Every word should serve a purpose
- Characters should feel real
- Endings should be earned
